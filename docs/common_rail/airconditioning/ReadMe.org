* Air Conditioning
  The A/C compressor control on the Audi 8P / CJAA-CBEA uses a clutchless variable compressor (N280)
  whose PWM signal is generated by the climate control module (Climatronic/J255).  The EDC17 does
  not directly drive the compressor. Without the climate module the ECM cannot turn the compressor
  on by itself.

** Thoughts on Approach
   Keep the factory Toyota AC amplifier from the 1983 Pickup as the safety and enable controller.
   Have an external/custom controller generate the 500 Hz PWM that the N280 valve needs.

   - Toyota amplifier
     - Reads its original evaporator thermistor, pressure switches, blower interlock, and AC switch.
     - Its clutch-output wire is the master “compressor allowed” signal.
     - Under normal operation it should stay continuously on because the custom controller keeps the
       evaporator from reaching the icing point.
     - If something fails and the evaporator gets too cold, the amplifier opens its output and the
       custom controller drops PWM to 0.

   - Custom Controller
     - Has its own evaporator temperature sensor.
     - Runs a slow control loop that targets ~2–4 °C (just above freezing).
     - Outputs 500 Hz PWM to the N280 valve only while the Toyota amplifier clutch line is ON.
     - When the amplifier output goes low, PWM is forced to 0 immediately.

   Idle-up from the Toyota amplifier is not required. My guess is that the CBEA has enough low-rpm
   torque and the PWM compressor ramps in gradually. The amplifier’s RPM interlock (it waits for RPM
   to rise before enabling the clutch output) may need to be spoofed or bypassed so the clutch line
   can close, or the idle adjustment knob turned down low enough.

*** CAN
    The climate request the EDC17 listens to is mClima_1 (0x5E0, 100 ms).  For basic operation the
    custom controller only needs to set the compressor-request bit (CL1_Kompressor) and a plausible
    ambient temperature. Other signals can stay at 0.  Optional: CL1_Kompr_Last (Byte 4 of 0x5E0)
    reports compressor torque load in Nm (0.25 Nm/bit, 0–63.5 Nm). Sending a small fixed value or a
    value scaled from PWM duty lets the ECM compensate idle; leaving it at 0 is also fine.  Once a
    valid 0x5E0 is sent, the ECM includes AC load in its fan calculation. Both the hardwired fan PWM
    pin and the MO5_Luefter percentage (Byte 5 of mMotor_5 / 0x480, 0.4 %/bit) will reflect
    that. The custom controller can then either let the ECM drive the fans or read MO5_Luefter and
    drive the SPALs itself.  The ECM only broadcasts status bits (for example MO5_KlimaKompr in
    mMotor_5 / 0x480).  It does not output a PWM command over CAN.

*** Notes
    - N280 is a PWM-controlled regulator valve, not a simple clutch. Frequency is typically 500 Hz.
    - Minimum controllable duty cycle is usually around 30 %, need to test.
    - The Toyota amplifier’s evaporator thermistor remains connected so it can still act as a hard
      anti-icing backup.
    - Binary/Pressure switch stays in the Toyota's amplifier circuit.
    - The amplifier’s RPM interlock is the main item that may still need a bypass or spoof, I don't
      know yet I need to test.
    - Push comes to shove we can see if there's a traditional clutch type compressor that fits on
      the CBEA and ditch the PWM controlled N280 haha
