* Steering
  The Audi 8P donor does not have a belt-driven hydraulic power steering pump on the engine. I need
  a solution for this swap.

** EHPS Pump Options
   Electric hydraulic power steering pump (EHPS) solution is being considered for the swap.  Several
   EHPS are being considered. The primary selection criteria is pressure output (PSI) higher
   capacity pumps from heavier vehicles (Dodge/Jeep platforms) are preferred because they can better
   support larger tires and off-road use.

*** Current Candidates
    - Jeep JL/JT EHPS
    - Dodge/Ram EHPS units (heavier-duty variants)
    - Dodge Challenger/Charger
    - Other high-output EHPS pumps from similar platforms (as research continues)

*** Main Challenge: CAN Bus Control
    All of these pumps require specific CAN messages (primarily vehicle speed, steering rate/angle,
    others) to enable and modulate assist. Without the correct messages the pump will either refuse
    to run or fall back to a limited default mode.

    Best-case scenario: the required messages have already been reverse engineered and publicly
    documented.

    If not, reverse engineering is still feasible, but only practical when a complete donor vehicle
    (or at least the full module set) is available for live CAN logging.

*** Pump
    Pumps are available in the US. I'm looking at some from Audi Q5s and A4s 8K0145153F, but more
    research is needed.

    After some research (not a lot) I found that almost any pump fits the ears on the bracket. Ended
    up going with a 2006 Audi A4 gasoline engine power steering pump (125mm pulley) mainly due to
    being able to remove it without having to remove the pulley at the junkyard as I forgot my set
    of triple squares otherwise I would've gone with a 2007 Volkswagen Beetle which has a smaller
    pulley than the A4 pump. Both have a banjo fitting for the high pressure line which is desired
    for ease of installation.

*** Results
    The 03L903143H bracket and accessories (including the 2006 A4 power steering pump) mounted and
    the belt length was sorted. The problem showed up when the engine was checked in the 1983 Toyota
    Pickup engine bay.  Using the 3D scan CAD models, the AC compressor sits roughly 1" into the
    frame rail and the serpentine belt path also passes through the frame. There is no practical way
    to clear the rail with this bracket and the AC compressor in its stock location on the H ear.
    The H bracket itself is usable on the CBEA/CJAA, but it does not package in the RN38 engine bay
    without major frame modification. The Toyota pickup frame is simply too narrow at 23.25 inches
    wide.
